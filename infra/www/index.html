How it works

Three steps. Then it just runs.

No integration project, no data migration, no rules to write. Upload what your IT team has already written and the assistant starts answering from it.

01

Upload your runbooks

PDFs, Word documents, Markdown — whatever your IT team already keeps. Diagrams and screenshots inside them are read too, so a setting shown only in a picture is still findable.

02

Your team just asks

Plain English, any hour. They can paste a screenshot of the error instead of describing it — the assistant reads the dialog and works from that.

03

Humans get the hard ones

When the answer isn't in your documentation, it stops and offers to escalate. Your IT team receives the full conversation by email, so nobody re-explains anything.

Why it's different

Built to be trusted,
not just to sound confident.

A wrong answer costs your team more time than no answer at all. Three decisions follow from that, and they shape the whole product.

It says "I don't know"

If your runbooks don't cover it, the assistant tells the user and offers a human instead of inventing a plausible procedure. Confidently wrong is the failure this product exists to avoid.

Your data stays yours

Every workspace is isolated inside the database itself, not merely by application code. One company's documents and conversations are unreachable from another's, enforced by PostgreSQL rather than trusted to a filter.

It won't mix your systems up

Two VPN clients, two mail setups — the assistant picks the runbook that applies and follows that one. It will not blend steps from two documents into a procedure that works for neither.
